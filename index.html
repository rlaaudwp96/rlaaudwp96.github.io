Food Residue Classification

R · Machine Learning · Random Forest · In progress

Can pesticide residue profiles predict what kind of food a sample is? Graduate research in Cal State East Bay’s OSCAR program.

The question

Can the pesticide residues found in a food sample predict its broad food group (plant-based, animal-based, or prepared/beverage)? Does accuracy change by region, season, or time period?

Method

Started from FDA Total Diet Study lab results for 2017–2022, where one food sample is tested for hundreds of pesticides. I reshaped them to one row per food sample, log-transformed the concentrations, grouped 18 food categories into three groups, and trained a 300-tree Random Forest on an 80/20 stratified split. I compare accuracy against a majority-class baseline, and also check balanced accuracy, Cohen’s kappa, and per-class recall. A temporal test trains on 2017–2018 and predicts 2019–2022.

Status

Ongoing. I’m scheduled to present the findings at the CSUEB Student Showcase in April 2027, and I’ll add results here afterward.

Health Insurance Costs

R · Regression

Predicting insurance costs with log-transformed regression and interaction modeling.

The question

Insurers lose money by underpricing risk and lose customers by overpricing it. Which traits drive an individual’s medical charges, and can they be predicted in a way people can interpret?

Method

Used 1,338 policyholders. Log-transformed charges to tame right skew, added age² and BMI² terms, and tested interactions such as BMI × smoker. Compared models with AIC, adjusted R², and 70/30 train/test RMSE.

What I found

Smoking is the largest driver. For a 27-year-old man with a BMI of 28, predicted charges jump from $2,757 to $22,148, nearly 8 times higher. BMI raises costs much more steeply for smokers.

Egg Production by Season

Statistics · ANOVA

Does US egg output really drop in some seasons? ANOVA on 2023 USDA data.

The question

Egg prices swung in 2023. Was it a seasonal production drop, or something else?

Method

Cleaned 2023 USDA NASS data, mapped months to four meteorological seasons, checked assumptions with Q-Q plots, and ran a one-way ANOVA on 216 state-month observations.

What I found

No significant difference between seasons (F = 0.017, p = 0.997). Output stayed steady, so the price spikes were likely driven by factors like avian flu, demand, or feed costs rather than seasonal supply.

EFL Promotion Analysis

R · SQL · BigQuery · ML

Which performance metrics are linked to promotion from the English Championship?

The question

Across 20 seasons of the EFL Championship, which team-performance metrics are associated with winning promotion?

Method

Imported a summary table from BigQuery, cleaned it in R, and explored a bimodal shot-accuracy distribution caused by changes in how the data was collected. Modeled points with regression, then promotion with logistic regression and random forest.

What I found

Built a cleaned team-season table and compared six performance indicators against points, with variable importance from the random forest. See the report on GitHub for the full results.

FAA Bird Strikes

R · ggplot2 · EDA

Where and when bird strikes happen, California vs. other states.

The question

Which flight phases and times of day see the most bird strikes, and does California differ from the rest of the US?

Method

Cleaned the FAA bird strike data from the openintro package, grouped states into California vs. other, and compared collision frequency by flight phase and time of day with ggplot2.

What I found

Most strikes happen during approach and landing, mostly in daytime. California’s strikes are spread more evenly across phases, while other states concentrate heavily in approach.
